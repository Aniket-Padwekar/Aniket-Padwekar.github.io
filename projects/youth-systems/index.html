Europe's youth systems

Which countries grow the most elite footballers, and where do those players learn the game? Explore 500 elite players across ten seasons, by nation, club and age.

Data last updated 9 October 2026. This is a one-time snapshot, not a live feed: match ratings run through the 2025/26 season and squad lists through the 2026 World Cup. Player photos load live from Wikimedia Commons.

Map

Countries are shaded by the measure you choose. The United Kingdom is one shape on this map and carries England's figures. Nations outside Europe appear in the ranking below.

How to read this

  • Sample. 500 players ranked by average Sofascore match rating across the big five leagues and the Champions League, 2016/17 to 2025/26. It measures elite output, not a hit rate for every child who entered a system, except in the youth-Euro table.
  • Trained in. Country of the youth club a player was at by age 15, from Wikipedia youth-career lists (448 of 500 players placed). First youth team and final nation come from Wikidata.
  • Uncertainty. Counts are small for most nations. Differences between nations close together are within noise; larger gaps hold up when the players are resampled.
  • Not covered. Ratings favor some positions, youth lists are incomplete, and academy regions inside a country are not available.
  • Sources. Sofascore, Wikidata, Wikipedia, FotMob (market values), Wikimedia Commons (photos).
